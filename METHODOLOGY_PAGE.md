## Hero
> ## From a fuzzy opportunity to a plan you can fund.
> Most game features die in production because teams build before they know what's worth building. Our method takes an opportunity through two cycles of *diverge-then-converge* thinking — first to decide **what** to make, then to prove **how** — with a **gate at every stage** so leadership only greenlights what's been validated. The output isn't a deck. It's a **prioritized development plan.**

---

## The four stages

**Start — Opportunity.** Every engagement begins with a real opportunity or problem worth solving, not a solution looking for a justification.

**1 · Discover — _Why._**
We diverge to understand the player and the business. Goals, personas, service blueprints, and documentation of how things actually work today. *(Owner, Designer, and Researcher work as one team from here.)*

**2 · Define — _What._**
We converge on the concept worth pursuing — made tangible as concept art, wireframes, and storyboards the whole team can rally behind.

> **🟢 Gate.** Go / no-go on evidence, not opinion. Nothing advances until the *what* is clear.

**3 · Develop — _How._**
We diverge on execution, integrating every discipline that touches the player — design, art, audio, engineering, production, QA, analytics, marketing, legal — through consultation and prototyping.

**4 · Deliver — _Proof._**
We converge on what's proven. Real **users** at the center, tested against analytics, cost, risk, and team capability — so you know it works *and* that you can build it.

> **🟢 Gate.** Only what survives validation moves forward.

**Output — A prioritized Development Plan.**
What to build, in what order, with cost and risk understood. A plan a greenlight committee can actually fund.

---

## Why it matters (the buyer payoff)
> **The gates are the point.** A production budget is the most expensive way to discover you built the wrong thing. By forcing a go/no-go on evidence at each gate, the method moves risk *forward* — to cheap prototypes and player tests — and keeps it out of full production. You spend your dev dollars on what's been proven, in priority order.

## Presentation notes (for the site)
- **Lead simple, let the detail be the proof.** On the homepage, show only the four stage labels + gates (Discover → Define → ▸gate▸ → Develop → Deliver → Plan). Reserve the full, icon-dense diagram for *this* page and the sales deck, where its rigor reads as credibility rather than clutter.
- **Lean into the gates as the differentiator.** Most "process" slides are linear arrows. The traffic-light gates are what make this a *decision* framework, not a workflow — name them and sell them.
- **Make the output concrete.** "A prioritized development plan" is the tangible thing a buyer takes away — say it everywhere the method appears.
