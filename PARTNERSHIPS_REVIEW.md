# Digital Experiments — Website Review (Partnerships Manager Lens)

**Reviewer frame:** Partnerships / developer-relations manager at a major game publisher, evaluating Digital Experiments (DXP) as an external UX partner to bring into studio teams.
**Date:** 2026-06-11
**Method note:** The live site (`www.digitalexperiments.com`) was not directly reachable from this environment (network allowlist + Squarespace bot-blocking), so this review is reconstructed from indexed page content across the homepage, `/new-about`, `/the-team`, `/contact`, and the `/blog` case studies (SciPlay/Jackpot Party, Project Copernicus). It is a **positioning, content, and structure** review — not a pixel-level visual critique. Items marked _[verify on live]_ should be confirmed against the rendered site.

---

## 1. The one-line verdict

There is a **real, fundable business** buried in this site — 25 years of game-UX pedigree, recognizable studio logos (Activision Blizzard / WoW, SciPlay, Mythical Games, 38 Studios), and a sharp, ownable POV ("why UX fails on major game teams"). But the site **sells the founder's résumé, not the buyer's outcome.** As a publisher partnerships manager, I would not yet have enough to forward this internally, because I can't answer the three questions my VP will ask: *What did they actually move (numbers)? Who at my tier vouches for them? What does a first engagement cost and look like?*

**Would I take the intro call?** Yes — on pedigree alone.
**Would I put them in front of a studio GM?** Not from this site. It reads small, generalist, and unquantified.

## 3. What it's MISSING (the gaps that block a "yes")

Ordered by how much each one costs DXP a deal.

1. **Quantified outcomes.** Every case study describes *activity* ("helped them understand the issues," "upskilled the team") and *zero results.* A publisher buys retention, conversion, ARPDAU, D1/D7, review-score lift, dev-efficiency. Without a single number, this reads as effort, not impact.
2. **Third-party validation.** No testimonial quotes, no named references, no logo wall presented *as proof* (logos are buried in body copy, not displayed). I need a quote with a name and title from someone at SciPlay/Mythical I could call.
3. **A defined engagement model.** I can't tell what buying DXP *is*: a workshop? an embedded UX lead? a fixed-scope audit? How long, what deliverables, what does week 1 look like? Enterprise buyers need a shaped, low-risk first step.
4. **Bench depth.** Two people on the team page reads as "a consultant," not "a partner who can staff my AAA team." Either show the extended network/associates or explicitly position the boutique model as a feature.
5. **Enterprise-readiness signals.** No mention of NDA/confidentiality posture, IP handling, or experience working inside AAA security/producer constraints. This is table stakes for unreleased-title work.
6. **A services taxonomy.** "Workshops and consultation" is vague. Name the products: UX audit, usability/playtest research, player research, team training/upskilling, UX design-ops/process, heuristic review. Let me match my problem to your SKU.
7. **A forwardable asset.** No capabilities deck / one-page PDF I can drop into an internal email. The way partnerships actually happens is *me selling you to my org* — give me ammo.
8. **Focused positioning.** The site straddles game UX **and** "Brand & Video Development" **and** generic B2C. Two homepages (`/` and `/home`) point at unfinished IA and a diffused offer. Specialists win enterprise UX work; generalists get filtered out.
9. **Conflict-of-interest clarity.** The founder also runs **Unleashed Games** (a game studio) and a separate personal site. A publisher's first worry: *is my UX partner also a competitor who'll see my roadmap?* The site never addresses the relationship. Silence here is a risk flag, not a neutral.

## 5. What would SELL me (publisher partnerships lens)

If these existed, I'd forward DXP internally the same day:

1. **One headline metric per case study.** "Cut new-player drop-off at SciPlay's Jackpot Party by X%." "Raised tutorial completion by Y points." Even directional/anonymized numbers beat none.
2. **A nameable, repeatable methodology.** Consultancies sell *IP.* Give the process a name and a diagram (e.g., a "UX Maturity Ladder" or "Define → Design → Deploy" loop the site already hints at). It signals this is a system, not a personality.
3. **A shaped, low-risk pilot.** A fixed-fee, 1–2 week **UX audit** or **team workshop** with a defined deliverable. Easy for me to expense and to sell internally as a test before a bigger commitment.
4. **References I can call** — two named contacts at studios at my tier.
5. **Proof you can operate inside AAA.** A line on NDAs, embedding with producers, working across design/product/eng, and handling unreleased IP.
6. **A forwardable capabilities deck** (PDF).
7. **Conflict cleared in one sentence.** "DXP partners with studios; we don't ship competing titles" — or an explicit, transparent description of the Unleashed relationship.

## 7. BRANDING SHIFT — recommendations

The current brand under-sells the substance. Five moves, in priority order:

1. **Anchor the name to the niche.** "Digital Experiments" is generic and **collides** with web "digital experimentation"/CRO/A-B-testing tooling — it's invisible in search and signals nothing about *games.* You don't necessarily need to rename, but the name should **never travel alone.** Adopt a permanent descriptor lockup: **"Digital Experiments — Game UX & Player Research."** If a rename is on the table, choose something that says *game UX maturity* on contact.

2. **Narrow to one ideal customer: large game studios & publishers raising UX maturity.** Today the offer spans game UX, brand, video, and generic B2C. Pick the lane where the pedigree is unbeatable (game UX) and make everything else secondary or sub-branded. Enterprise buyers hire specialists.

3. **Shift the voice from craft to business outcomes.** Move from "we do UX/UI" (a feature) to the language my CFO understands: **retention, conversion, LTV, review scores, dev efficiency, fewer post-launch fire drills.** UX is the means; revenue and retention are the brand promise.

4. **Productize the expertise into named IP.** A named **methodology/framework** (e.g., a UX Maturity model, the "Define → Design → Deploy" loop) plus tiered offers (Audit / Workshop / Embedded) turns a founder's reputation into a *company* a procurement team can buy. This is the single highest-leverage brand move.

5. **Elevate the visual & proof system from "portfolio" to "partner."** The current Squarespace/portfolio feel reads boutique-local (a phone number and a Pawtucket address as the primary contact reinforces this). Re-center the brand spine on **logos → metrics → named quotes → forwardable deck.** Same assets, repackaged as enterprise credibility.

**Net:** Keep the founder story — it's the crown jewel — but reframe the brand so it sells *the studio's outcome and a repeatable system,* not *one expert's history.* That's the difference between "interesting consultant" and "partner I can fund."
